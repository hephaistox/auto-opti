#+title: Sample
Sample is a set of function used to manipulate samples.  
* Quickstart
Let's say you have a set of numerical data =d=, the sampling consists in a lot of different functions, for instance you can compute the average with:
#+begin_src clojure
(require '[auto-opti.sample :as opti-sample])

(-> [32 73 30 41 58 39 55 24 63 81 62 68 40 70 51 16]
    opti-sample/average)
;; => 803/16
#+end_src

#+RESULTS:
: 803/16

* Usage
** Standard characteristic function
Many different functions are available to characterize the sample:
#+begin_src clojure
(require '[auto-opti.sample :as opti-sample])

((juxt opti-sample/average opti-sample/variance opti-sample/standard-deviation opti-sample/median opti-sample/midrange opti-sample/n)
          (-> (concat
               [32 73 30 41 58 39 55 24 63 81 62 68 40 70 51 16]
               [6 10 16 45 57 69 78 4 64 83 63 70 80 7 69 13 0 42 7 99 84 6 48 46 94 44 41 21 43])))
;; => [704/15 717.3511111111115 26.78341111791236 46 49.5 45]
#+end_src

#+RESULTS:
: [704/15 717.3511111111115 26.78341111791236 46 49.5 45]

** Frequencies
=freq= create a sample by observing the data and group them in sampling intervals.
#+begin_src clojure
(require '[auto-opti.sample :as opti-sample])

(-> (concat
     [32 73 30 41 58 39 55 24 63 81 62 68 40 70 51 16]
     [6 10 16 45 57 69 78 4 64 83 63 70 80 7 69 13 0 42 7 99 84 6 48 46 94 44 41 21 43])
    (opti-sample/freq 10))
;; => {0 6, 70 4, 20 2, 60 7, 50 4, 40 9, 90 2, 30 3, 10 4, 80 4}
#+end_src

#+RESULTS:
: {0 6, 70 4, 20 2, 60 7, 50 4, 40 9, 90 2, 30 3, 10 4, 80 4}

** erf
The error function ([[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error_function][erf]]) approximation:
#+begin_src clojure
(require '[auto-opti.sample :as opti-sample])

(opti-sample/erf 1)
;; => 0.8427006897475899
#+end_src

#+RESULTS:
: 0.8427006897475899
